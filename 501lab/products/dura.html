501 Lab / Project DURA
Biomedical R&D · In development

Adaptive dural substitute research program.

Project DURA explores a next-generation dural repair concept designed to preserve a protective barrier after neurosurgery while enabling controlled tissue integration over time.

Problem

Dural repair has to solve two needs at once.

A dural substitute must help maintain a reliable barrier against cerebrospinal fluid leakage, while also supporting healing and integration with surrounding tissue. Those needs can conflict when a material is optimized only for immediate closure or only for tissue ingrowth.

Project DURA is being developed around a staged architecture: maintain barrier function first, then progressively enable a regeneration-facing interface as healing proceeds.

Design principles

Designed around the surgical workflow.

The program is intentionally being developed without assuming that surgeons should adopt an entirely new implantation workflow.

01

Barrier first

The early post-operative period prioritizes physical protection and sealing performance.

02

Controlled transition

The tissue-facing interface is intended to become available progressively rather than all at once.

03

Workflow compatibility

The concept is being developed with conventional surgical handling and fixation constraints in mind.

Development plan

What needs to be proven next

  • Prototype manufacturability and repeatability
  • Barrier and leakage resistance under representative loading
  • Controlled opening or degradation behavior
  • Mechanical integrity during the healing window
  • Biocompatibility and tissue-response evaluation
  • Usability within a realistic surgical workflow
Program note
Research-stage technology. Project DURA is not a cleared or approved medical device and has not been clinically validated. Information on this page describes an R&D program and design hypothesis, not clinical performance.

501 Lab avoids presenting exploratory biomedical work as established medical evidence.
