501 Lab / Project MIND
Digital wellbeing · In development

A low-friction mental wellbeing support product.

For adults experiencing low mood who find it hard to maintain everyday routines, Project MIND is a wellbeing app concept built around short check-ins, simple routine cues, and links to appropriate support resources.

Open interactive prototype
Product direction

Support continuity before a difficult day becomes a lost week.

The product concept focuses on reducing friction: short check-ins, routine anchors, gentle reminders, and a clear view of recent patterns rather than long-form journaling or complex self-tracking.

It is designed as a wellbeing support tool. It does not diagnose depression, replace professional care, or claim to prevent or treat a mental disorder.

Intended users and pain point

For adults struggling to sustain routines during low-mood periods

When energy and motivation are low, lengthy journals and complicated habit trackers can become another burden. Project MIND is being designed around short check-ins, simple action prompts, and a low-pressure routine view.

Its scope is self-management support. It is not a depression diagnosis, prevention, or treatment service.

Planned Claude API workflow

Optional, safety-aware content assistance

  • Draft short, plain-language check-in questions and gentle habit prompts.
  • Generate optional weekly reflections only from user-approved check-in data, with clear opt-in.
  • Automate internal review of tone and content templates for safety and consistency.
  • Keep medical diagnoses and crisis decision-making outside the model's scope; prioritize verified support resources.
Product principles

Small actions, low burden, clear boundaries.

The product is being designed to be useful without turning mental wellbeing into another high-effort tracking task.

Safety-aware escalation

Planned capabilities

Initial product scope

  • Brief daily mood and functioning check-ins
  • Personal routine anchors and gentle reminders
  • Simple trend views rather than dense analytics
  • User-defined support contacts and resource shortcuts
  • Privacy-conscious local-first design where practical
  • Clear safety messaging for crisis or high-risk situations
